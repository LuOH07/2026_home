Compact is only the beginning.
Engineering ISHahll TnpB into a more targetable and controllable
The Challenge
Genome editing gets harder beyond familiar hosts
Genome editing has transformed synthetic biology, but many non-model microorganisms remain difficult to engineer.
Large editing systems can impose burdens on delivery, expression and host compatibility, creating a need for more compact and adaptable genome-editing tools.
This led us to TnpB-a remarkably compact RNA-guided nuclease of approximately 400 amino acids.
The Twist - But small does not mean flexible.
ISHahl1 TnpB offered a promising compact starting point, but its usefulness came with a critical limitation: a strict preference for the 5'-CTAC-3' TAM.
If the required TAM is absent beside a desired genomic position, even an active nuclease cannot access that target.
An editor can cut well - and still be unable to cut where we need it.
Our Answer – Sting
From a compact nuclease to a usable platform.
We developed our project around three connected questions:
BUILD - Can ISHahl1 TnpB function as a versatile bacterial genome-engineering platform?
We established and evaluated TnpB-based genome editing, interference and base-editingfunctions in bacterial systems.
EXPAND - Can we increase where TnpB can act?
We engineered TAM recognition through structure-guided analysis, variant generation, fluorescence-based selection, TAM profiling and functional validation.
CONTAIN - Can the organisms we engineer remain controllable?
We further explored TnpB as a component of a genetic biocontainment system, connecting genome engineering with responsible use.
Engineering Targetability
From structure to function, we engineered TnpB to recognize more targets.
UNDERSTAND
Identify residues potentially involved in TAM recognition through structural analysis.
DIVERSIFY
Generate variants around an engineerable recognition landscape.
SCREEN
Use fluorescence-based selection and TAM profiling to identify altered recognition.
VALIDATE
Test whether candidates retain intracellular cleavage and genome-editing function.
From Design to Evidence
Our results address three progressively larger questions.
Can we build a functional TnpB platform? Genome editing interference base editing
Can we engineer where TnpB can act? Directed evolution- TAM profiling functional validation
Can TnpB also help us retain control? Genetic target selection-killing efficiency- biocontainment
Why It Matters
More targets. More possibilities. More responsibility.
A compact editor becomes more useful when it can access the genomic positions that matter.
Broader TAM recognition could increase the engineering freedom available in bacterial and non-model hosts, while biocontainment reminds us that greater capability must be accompanied by greater control.
