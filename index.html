Genome editing gets harder beyond Familiar Host
Genome editing has transformed synthetic biology, yet adapting editing tools across bacterial hosts remains challenging.
Large systems can complicate delivery and expression, motivating the development of compact and adaptable alternatives.
But small does NOT mean flexible.
ISHahl1 TnpB, a remarkably compact RNA-guided nuclease of approximately 400 amino acids, offered a promising choice.
However, its broader applicability was limited by a strict preference for
the 5'-CTAC-3' TAM.
An editor can cut well
and still be unable to cut where we need it.
That is why we introduce
Small TnpB-based Innovative Nuclease
toolkit for bacterial Genome editing.
From a compact nuclease to a functional, targetable, and explainable platform.

Our platform allows
More functions. More targets. More understanding.
STING integrates bacterial genome editing and regulation, expanded TAM recognition, and computational guidance.
From bioinformatics-based design, through data-driven mutant prioritization, to molecular-level explanation, it offers a clearer path from a compact nuclease to a more flexible and interpretable genome-editing toolbox.
